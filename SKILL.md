# REALITY RESTAGED

Documentary memory. Surreal stage.

Reality Restaged is not a generic surrealism style.

It is a visual system for transforming real documentary photographs into cinematic surreal tableaux that feel as though they belong to the same artistic universe.

The objective is not:

“make this photograph surreal.”

The objective is:

“preserve the reality inside the photograph, then rebuild the world around it according to the Reality Restaged visual language.”

A successful image should feel like:

a real photograph of a world that could never exist.

────────────────────

# 1. THE REALITY RESTAGED VISUAL DNA

Every new work should be built from the following visual equation:

DOCUMENTARY SUBJECT

+

RADICALLY SIMPLIFIED STAGE

+

LARGE CONTROLLED COLOR FIELDS

+

ONE IMPOSSIBLE RELATIONSHIP

+

STRONG SCALE OR SPATIAL TENSION

+

NEGATIVE SPACE

+

ANALOG PHOTOGRAPHIC REALISM

The subject may change.

The country may change.

The culture may change.

The surreal mechanism may change.

The composition may change.

But the visual grammar should remain recognizable.

The goal is:

SAME UNIVERSE.
DIFFERENT WORLDS.

────────────────────

# 2. REALITY FIRST

Before transforming anything, understand what must survive from the source photograph.

Identify:

## HERO

What is the visual anchor?

It may be:

- a person
- a group of people
- an animal
- a vehicle
- an object
- a gesture
- distinctive clothing
- a cultural symbol

## RELATIONSHIP

What is happening between the important elements?

Examples:

- human and animal
- observer and performer
- fisherman and waiting animals
- rider and landscape
- person and shadow
- group and ritual
- vehicle and traveler
- subject and architecture

## DOCUMENTARY DETAIL

What details make this photograph specific rather than generic?

Examples:

- clothing
- hats
- cloaks
- jewelry
- tools
- flowers
- staffs
- vehicles
- ritual gestures
- cultural symbols
- local architecture
- characteristic colors

## ACCIDENT

Look for useful photographic accidents:

- strange overlap
- unexpected shadow
- unusual spacing
- accidental symmetry
- an animal entering the frame
- partial obstruction
- unusual scale
- awkward gesture
- color coincidence

These accidents may contain the surreal idea already hidden inside the photograph.

Do not erase them automatically.

────────────────────

# 3. DISTILL BEFORE DESIGNING

Reduce the documentary photograph to one visual sentence.

Use:

SUBJECT
+
RELATIONSHIP
+
CONTRADICTION

Examples:

A fisherman and two animals wait around a single fish.

Several jumping figures attempt to reach an observer positioned impossibly high above them.

A traveler pulls an object while an impossible shadow dominates the landscape.

A rider and animal cross a landscape whose geometry no longer obeys reality.

If the concept requires a paragraph to explain:

SIMPLIFY IT. ────────────────────

# 4. RECOMPOSITION IS THE DEFAULT

For every NEW Reality Restaged image:

DO NOT simply preserve the original composition and add surreal objects.

DO NOT merely replace the sky.

DO NOT merely recolor the photograph.

DO NOT apply a surreal filter.

RECOMPOSE THE WORLD.

Unless the user explicitly asks to preserve an existing composition, redesign at least one major spatial relationship.

Possible transformations include:

- reposition the hero within the frame
- enlarge foreground subjects
- reduce distant figures
- alter the horizon
- create extreme foreground/background tension
- isolate subjects from environmental clutter
- change the distance between figures
- transform the relationship between human and landscape
- transform the relationship between animal and human
- transform the relationship between body and shadow
- turn architecture into a spatial device
- create monumental negative space

The final composition must feel deliberately staged.

It should NOT feel like:

“the same photograph with surreal elements added.”

────────────────────

# 5. RADICALLY SIMPLIFY THE ENVIRONMENT

Reality Restaged usually removes more than it adds.

Documentary backgrounds are source material, not sacred layouts.

Preserve environmental information only when it contributes to:

- identity
- geography
- culture
- story
- relationship
- surreal mechanism

Otherwise simplify it aggressively.

Replace unnecessary environmental complexity with:

- large matte walls
- flat ground planes
- clean horizons
- monumental color fields
- simple architectural surfaces
- minimal sea surfaces
- controlled geometric spatial divisions
- isolated landscape fragments
- large areas of negative space

Do not reproduce every building, tree, car, sign, cloud, chair, object, or passerby simply because it appears in the original photograph.

Ask:

WHAT CAN BE REMOVED?

before asking:

WHAT CAN BE ADDED?

────────────────────

# 6. LARGE COLOR FIELDS ARE A SIGNATURE

Large areas of controlled saturated color are a defining part of the Reality Restaged visual language.

They are not optional decoration.

For NEW compositions, actively seek opportunities to transform complex scenery into broad fields of color.

Prefer approximately:

2 DOMINANT COLORS

+

1 SUPPORTING COLOR

At least one dominant color should usually occupy a substantial portion of the frame.

Useful color relationships include:

- cobalt blue × vermilion red
- deep cyan × terracotta
- ochre yellow × burgundy
- orange × black
- cream × deep blue
- teal × warm red
- dusty yellow × dark crimson
- muted sand × saturated blue

Do NOT mechanically use the same palette for every work.

The palette should respond to the documentary source while still producing a strong graphic contrast.

Color should organize:

- foreground
- background
- subject
- architecture
- negative space

Color must do structural work.

Do not use color merely to make the image attractive.

Avoid:

- rainbow palettes
- weak muddy backgrounds
- excessive gradients
- many equally dominant colors
- random colorful objects
- generic teal-and-orange cinematic grading

When a composition feels visually weak:

SIMPLIFY THE COLOR STRUCTURE BEFORE ADDING MORE OBJECTS.

────────────────────

# 7. ONE IMPOSSIBLE RELATIONSHIP

The central surreal principle is:

ONE IMPOSSIBLE RELATIONSHIP.

Not:

MANY SURREAL OBJECTS.

The strongest Reality Restaged images should contain one impossible relationship that changes the meaning of the original documentary moment.

Possible mechanisms include:

## SCALE

Change the scale relationship.

Examples:

- an animal becomes larger than the human observing it
- a shadow becomes larger than its owner
- a person becomes tiny inside a monumental environment
- a foreground subject becomes monumental against distant figures

## SPACE

Transform physical space.

Examples:

- the ground becomes a wall
- the sea becomes a table
- a landscape folds
- two incompatible spaces meet
- the horizon behaves like architecture

## ARCHITECTURE

Use architecture to restructure reality.

Examples:

- a wall interrupts a landscape
- an opening becomes an impossible threshold
- a natural structure becomes architectural
- an architectural plane divides two realities

## SHADOW

Change the relationship between body and shadow.

Examples:

- the shadow separates from the body
- the shadow becomes more dominant than the person
- the shadow reveals a different scale or identity

## REPETITION

Turn documentary movement into spatial rhythm.

Examples:

- a gesture repeats through space
- one action appears at several stages
- a group becomes a visual sequence

## GRAVITY

Alter expected physical orientation.

## REFLECTION

Allow a reflection to become another spatial reality.

## ABSENCE

Remove most of reality until only the documentary core remains.

The surrealism must change a RELATIONSHIP.

Do not simply place a strange object beside the subject.

────────────────────

# 8. THE STAGE

Reality Restaged should often feel as if reality has been reconstructed on an enormous theatrical stage.

Think:

TABLEAU

+

THEATRE

+

INSTALLATION

+

PHOTOGRAPHY

Useful spatial structures include:

- monumental walls
- clean floors
- isolated platforms
- simple horizons
- architectural cuts
- enormous flat surfaces
- water planes
- restrained openings
- large empty fields
- impossible spatial intersections

The stage should be simple enough that the documentary subject remains emotionally dominant.

Avoid over-designed environments.

The surreal world should feel monumental but visually quiet.

────────────────────

# 9. NEGATIVE SPACE

Negative space is one of the primary compositional tools of Reality Restaged.

Use it deliberately to create:

- silence
- scale
- isolation
- tension
- monumentality
- visual rhythm
- breathing room

Do not fill empty areas merely because they feel empty.

A large uninterrupted field of:

- blue
- red
- orange
- cream
- yellow
- black
- cyan
- terracotta

may be more powerful than another surreal object.

When uncertain:

REMOVE.

────────────────────

# 10. SCALE AS STORY

Scale distortion must express meaning.

Good uses include:

- animal larger than human
- shadow larger than body
- landscape overwhelming architecture
- clothing becoming architectural
- tiny human against monumental color
- oversized foreground subject facing a distant human
- repeated figures changing scale through depth

Bad use:

making something enormous only because “large objects look surreal.”

Scale must strengthen the story and the relationship.

────────────────────

# 11. MINIMUM TRANSFORMATION THRESHOLD

Before accepting a NEW Reality Restaged composition, ask:

IF THE SURREAL INTERVENTION DISAPPEARED,
WOULD THIS STILL LOOK MOSTLY LIKE THE ORIGINAL PHOTOGRAPH?

If YES:

THE TRANSFORMATION IS TOO WEAK.

Do not accept it.

Instead:

- recompose the scene
- simplify the environment
- strengthen the scale relationship
- strengthen the spatial contradiction
- enlarge the color fields
- increase negative space
- redesign the relationship between subject and world

The result must clearly feel like a newly constructed visual world.

Not a filtered photograph.

Not a lightly edited photograph.

Not a photograph with one surreal prop added.

A REALITY RESTAGED IMAGE MUST BE VISIBLY RESTAGED. ────────────────────

# 12. HUMAN FIGURES MUST REMAIN REAL

The world may become impossible.

The people must remain believable.

Preserve:

- realistic human height
- natural leg length
- natural torso length
- believable shoulder width
- realistic head-to-body ratio
- natural posture
- believable center of gravity
- authentic clothing structure
- natural skin texture
- realistic hands and limbs

Never vertically compress a person to make them fit the composition.

If more room is needed:

EXPAND THE WORLD.

DO NOT SHORTEN THE PERSON.

Avoid:

- oversized heads
- short legs
- compressed torsos
- miniature-looking adults
- mannequin-like bodies
- wax-like faces
- artificial poses

Human proportions should feel naturally elongated when appropriate to the original subject.

Do not sacrifice believable anatomy for graphic composition.

────────────────────

# 13. INDIVIDUAL PEOPLE MUST REMAIN INDIVIDUAL

When multiple real people appear, preserve their differences.

Check:

- facial structure
- age
- height
- body type
- skin texture
- expression
- posture
- clothing
- accessories
- hairstyle
- gesture

Avoid SAME-FACE SYNDROME.

Do not transform a group of real individuals into copies of one synthetic person.

When exact facial identity cannot be preserved reliably, prioritize believable individuality rather than generating visibly duplicated faces.

────────────────────

# 14. CULTURAL INTEGRITY

When the source photograph contains cultural, religious, ceremonial, traditional, or geographically specific details, preserve them carefully.

Protect:

- traditional clothing
- hats
- cloaks
- embroidery
- jewelry
- beads
- staffs
- crosses
- flowers
- tools
- ritual gestures
- culturally meaningful symbols
- characteristic materials
- culturally meaningful colors

Do not invent exotic details merely to make the scene appear more unusual.

Do not turn cultural identity itself into the surreal spectacle.

The principle is:

MAKE THE WORLD SURREAL.

NOT THE CULTURE.

The documentary specificity of the original photograph should remain recognizable inside the reconstructed world.

────────────────────

# 15. ANIMALS MUST REMAIN ANATOMICALLY BELIEVABLE

Animals may change scale.

Their anatomy should not become accidentally surreal.

Always check:

- number of heads
- number of limbs
- tail
- ears
- eyes
- mouth
- wings
- paws
- body connections
- posture

Avoid:

- duplicated heads
- missing tails
- extra limbs
- fused bodies
- accidental hybrids
- malformed wings
- unnecessary anthropomorphism

If surrealism requires an animal to become monumental:

CHANGE SCALE.

NOT ANATOMY.

────────────────────

# 16. PHOTOGRAPHIC REALISM

Reality Restaged should look PHOTOGRAPHED.

Not rendered.

Not illustrated.

Not painted.

Not like generic AI concept art.

Prefer:

- believable natural lighting
- realistic directional shadows
- natural skin texture
- realistic fabric
- subtle analog grain
- restrained sharpness
- soft highlight roll-off
- believable depth
- realistic photographic exposure
- slight optical imperfection
- controlled cinematic color
- subtle film character

The image may contain impossible space.

But the photographic surface should remain convincing.

Allow minor photographic imperfection.

Perfect digital surfaces often destroy photographic credibility.

The desired feeling is:

A DOCUMENTARY PHOTOGRAPH TAKEN INSIDE AN IMPOSSIBLE WORLD.

────────────────────

# 17. FILM TEXTURE

Film character should be subtle and integrated.

Prefer:

- fine analog grain
- slightly softened micro-contrast
- restrained saturation within strong color fields
- natural highlight behavior
- gentle tonal falloff
- slight optical softness
- realistic shadow density
- tactile fabric and skin

Do not simulate film by simply covering the image with obvious noise.

Do not over-process.

The image should feel cinematic because of:

LIGHT
+
COMPOSITION
+
COLOR
+
SPACE
+
TEXTURE

not because of a heavy preset.

────────────────────

# 18. AVOID THE GENERIC AI LOOK

Avoid:

- excessive sharpness
- HDR appearance
- plastic skin
- wax-like faces
- artificial cinematic glow
- excessive rim lighting
- glossy 3D surfaces
- hyper-detail everywhere
- perfect synthetic symmetry
- fantasy concept-art rendering
- painterly surfaces
- generic cinematic haze
- malformed hands
- duplicated faces
- distorted animals
- unnecessary visual effects

Do not make every surface spectacular.

Do not make every object perfectly polished.

Do not make every shadow dramatic.

Restraint is part of the visual identity.

When choosing between:

MORE SPECTACLE

and

MORE RESTRAINT

choose:

MORE RESTRAINT.

────────────────────

# 19. DO NOT TURN SYMBOLS INTO FORMULAS

Reality Restaged may sometimes use:

- doors
- windows
- mirrors
- spheres
- stairs
- flowers
- shadows
- birds
- openings
- geometric forms

But none of these are mandatory signatures.

Never automatically add:

A RED DOOR.

A MOON.

A SPHERE.

A WINDOW.

A MIRROR.

A GIANT SHADOW.

A GIANT ANIMAL.

These are possible mechanisms.

They are NOT the visual identity itself.

The style must be recognizable through:

- recomposition
- monumental color
- negative space
- scale
- spatial tension
- documentary realism
- cinematic staging

not through repeated props.

# 20. REFERENCE IMAGES — LEARN THE GRAMMAR, NOT THE SCENE

When Reality Restaged reference works or examples are available, treat them as STYLE EVIDENCE.

Study them for recurring visual decisions such as:

- ratio of subject to negative space
- size of dominant color fields
- saturation relationships
- foreground/background scale
- horizon placement
- human scale
- animal scale
- theatrical staging
- environmental simplification
- photographic texture
- surreal intensity
- visual silence
- relationship between documentary subject and impossible world

Extract the recurring visual grammar across MULTIPLE references.

Do not overfit to one reference image.

DO NOT copy:

- exact composition
- exact pose
- exact architecture
- exact surreal object
- exact palette
- exact placement
- exact background
- exact metaphor

TRANSFER THE VISUAL GRAMMAR.

DO NOT REPRODUCE THE SCENE.

The desired result is:

“This belongs beside the references.”

Not:

“This copies one of the references.”

# 21. MASTER LOCK MODE

When the user identifies an existing generated image as an approved version or says:

- use this version
- use this as the base
- use this as the master
- modify this image
- everything else stays the same
- only change this element
- do not change anything else

activate:

MASTER LOCK MODE.

Lock:

- composition
- camera angle
- perspective
- subject position
- human scale
- animal scale
- background
- horizon
- accepted color system
- accepted surreal mechanism
- lighting
- photographic texture

Modify ONLY what the user explicitly requests.

If the user says:

“Fix the fish tail.”

Fix the fish tail.

Do not redesign the sea.

If the user says:

“Change the face.”

Change the face.

Do not regenerate the background, clothing, proportions, or composition unless technically necessary.

An approved composition has priority over the default recomposition rules.

# 22. ASPECT RATIO CHANGES

When converting an APPROVED composition to another aspect ratio:

preserve the approved composition.

Prefer:

- extending the canvas
- revealing additional surrounding space
- careful outpainting

Think:

THE PHOTOGRAPHER USED A LARGER PIECE OF FILM.

Do not:

- shorten people
- squash animals
- stretch scenery
- crop important subjects
- move accepted elements
- alter accepted scale relationships
- unnecessarily regenerate the entire scene

Aspect-ratio conversion is NOT permission to redesign an approved image. ────────────────────

# 23. CONCEPT EXPLORATION

When the user requests ideas before generation, create genuinely different concepts.

By default, explore three different surreal mechanisms.

For example:

CONCEPT A — SCALE

Transform the size relationship between subjects.

CONCEPT B — SPACE

Fold, divide, interrupt, or reconstruct physical space.

CONCEPT C — RELATIONSHIP

Transform the relationship between people, animals, objects, architecture, or landscape.

The concepts must differ in visual logic.

Do not create:

- red door version
- blue door version
- yellow door version

and call them three concepts.

Different color is not necessarily a different idea.

# 24. DEFAULT CREATION WORKFLOW

For every NEW source photograph, follow this sequence:

STEP 1 — READ THE MEMORY

Understand what is actually happening in the documentary photograph.

STEP 2 — IDENTIFY THE HERO

Choose the primary visual anchor.

STEP 3 — IDENTIFY THE RELATIONSHIP

Determine the most meaningful interaction between the important elements.

STEP 4 — PROTECT DOCUMENTARY SPECIFICITY

Identify clothing, gestures, animals, objects, cultural details, and environmental clues that must survive.

STEP 5 — FIND THE ACCIDENT

Look for accidental photographic relationships that may contain the seed of the surreal idea.

STEP 6 — REMOVE VISUAL NOISE

Determine what can disappear without damaging the story.

STEP 7 — DISTILL THE STORY

Express the image as:

SUBJECT
+
RELATIONSHIP
+
CONTRADICTION

STEP 8 — CHOOSE ONE IMPOSSIBLE RELATIONSHIP

Choose one primary surreal mechanism.

STEP 9 — RECOMPOSE

Redesign at least one major relationship between subject and environment.

Do not preserve the original layout by default.

STEP 10 — BUILD THE STAGE

Transform the environment into a restrained theatrical spatial structure.

STEP 11 — SIMPLIFY AGGRESSIVELY

Replace unnecessary realistic scenery with clean planes, horizons, architecture, negative space, or monumental color.

STEP 12 — BUILD THE COLOR ARCHITECTURE

Prefer approximately:

2 DOMINANT COLORS
+
1 SUPPORTING COLOR

At least one dominant color should normally occupy substantial visual space when appropriate.

STEP 13 — CREATE SCALE OR SPATIAL TENSION

Ask whether scale, distance, depth, horizon, shadow, reflection, repetition, or architecture can strengthen the relationship.

STEP 14 — PRESERVE REAL PEOPLE

Check anatomy, height, posture, faces, clothing, and individual differences.

STEP 15 — PRESERVE CULTURAL MEMORY

Restore meaningful documentary and cultural details.

STEP 16 — CHECK ANIMALS

Correct anatomy before accepting the image.

STEP 17 — APPLY PHOTOGRAPHIC REALISM

Use believable light, shadow, skin, fabric, exposure, depth, and subtle analog film character.

STEP 18 — REMOVE DECORATION

Delete surreal elements that do not contribute to the central relationship.

STEP 19 — RUN THE MINIMUM TRANSFORMATION TEST

If the result still looks mostly like the original photograph:

RECOMPOSE AGAIN.

STEP 20 — RUN THE ONE-SECOND TEST

Check whether the image communicates immediately at phone-feed size.

STEP 21 — RUN THE REALITY RESTAGED SERIES TEST

Ask whether the image belongs to the same visual universe without copying an earlier composition.

# 25. ONE-SECOND TEST

Imagine the finished image displayed small on a phone.

Within approximately one second, the viewer should perceive:

ONE HERO

+

ONE IMPOSSIBLE RELATIONSHIP

+

ONE STRONG COLOR STRUCTURE

The image should have an immediate graphic silhouette and hierarchy.

If the surreal concept only becomes visible after zooming in:

STRENGTHEN IT.

If too many surreal ideas compete immediately:

SIMPLIFY IT.

If the image reads first as an ordinary travel photograph:

RECOMPOSE IT.
