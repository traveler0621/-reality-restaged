name: reality-restaged description: Transform documentary, travel, street, and human-centered photographs into restrained cinematic surreal tableaux while preserving the identity, cultural details, relationships, gestures, and photographic realism of the source image. Use when the user wants to reinterpret a real photograph as a surreal, minimal, staged, editorial, or cinematic image rather than merely apply a visual filter.

Reality Restaged

Transform documentary memory into a surreal stage.

The goal is not to make an image look “AI-generated.”

The goal is to create:

a photograph that feels as if it could have been captured in reality, even though the scene itself could never exist.

────────

1. Core Principle

Reality first. Surrealism second.

Always understand the source photograph before redesigning it.

The original photograph is not merely visual material. It contains:

• people
• gestures
• relationships
• cultural details
• clothing
• animals
• objects
• color accidents
• environmental clues
• documentary moments

Preserve the emotional and documentary core.

Transform the world around it.

────────

2. Read the Photograph

Before generating anything, identify three things.

HERO

Ask:

If only one thing could remain from this photograph, what should it be?

It may be:

• a person
• a group
• an animal
• a gesture
• a vehicle
• an object
• a distinctive piece of clothing
• a cultural symbol

This becomes the visual anchor.

RELATIONSHIP

Do not only ask what objects are present.

Ask:

What is happening between them?

Examples:

• a person follows an animal
• several people perform the same ritual
• animals wait for food
• a person pulls an object
• someone looks toward another person
• a rider crosses a landscape
• a person interacts with their own shadow

Preserve relationships whenever possible.

ACCIDENT

Look for accidental photographic moments:

• unusual overlap
• strange scale
• unexpected shadows
• animals entering the frame
• color coincidences
• awkward spacing
• partial obstruction
• unusual gestures

Documentary accidents are valuable.

Do not automatically “correct” them.

They may become the foundation of the surreal concept.

────────

3. Distill the Story

Reduce the photograph to one visual sentence:

subject + relationship/action + visual contradiction

Example:

> A man and a donkey walk toward an impossible exit.

> A fisherman and two animals wait around a single fish.

> A group of people jump toward an observer positioned impossibly high above them.

If the concept cannot be explained simply, it may contain too many ideas.

────────

4. One Impossible Thing

Prefer one strong surreal intervention over many decorative surreal elements.

Possible interventions include:

SCALE

Change the size relationship between subjects.

FOLD

Fold the landscape, sea, wall, or ground.

CUT

Cut or interrupt physical space.

PASSAGE

Create an impossible passage between spaces.

SHADOW

Allow a shadow to become independent from its subject.

MIRROR

Transform reflection into another reality.

REPETITION

Repeat a person or gesture to represent time or rhythm.

GRAVITY

Alter the expected physical orientation of an object.

MATERIAL

Transform one material into another spatial structure.

ABSENCE

Remove most of the original environment.

Choose the smallest intervention capable of producing the strongest idea.

────────

5. Do Not Use Surrealism as Decoration

Never add surreal objects merely because the composition feels empty.

Be especially cautious with:

• doors
• windows
• moons
• spheres
• stairs
• clouds
• mirrors
• flowers
• birds
• floating objects
• geometric sculptures

Every element must answer:

Why is this here?

If there is no meaningful answer, remove it.

────────

6. Stage the Reality

Reconstruct reality as a theatrical or cinematic stage.

Useful spatial structures include:

• large walls
• simple floors
• clear horizons
• architectural cuts
• platforms
• openings
• windows
• water surfaces
• mirrors
• monumental natural forms

Aim for the spatial feeling of:

tableau + theatre + photography + installation

The people should remain believable.

The world around them may become impossible.

────────

7. Composition

Prefer:

• strong horizontal composition
• cinematic framing
• large negative space
• clear visual hierarchy
• restrained number of subjects
• strong foreground/midground/background relationships

Common ratios:

• 16:9 for cinematic horizontal compositions
• 4:3 for editorial photographic compositions

Negative space is not empty.

It creates:

• scale
• silence
• tension
• isolation
• monumentality
• visual breathing room

When an element does not contribute to the concept, remove it.

────────

8. Scale

Scale distortion can be used when it supports the story.

Examples:

• animal larger than human
• shadow larger than human
• landscape larger than architecture
• clothing becoming architectural in scale
• tiny human inside a monumental field of color

Do not enlarge objects simply to make the image look surreal.

Scale must express a relationship.

────────

9. Human Proportions

Human figures must remain anatomically believable.

Preserve:

• natural height
• elongated realistic proportions
• correct leg length
• correct torso length
• realistic shoulder width
• natural posture
• believable center of gravity

Never compress a person vertically to make them fit the composition.

If additional space is required:

expand the canvas instead of shortening the human figure.

Avoid:

• oversized heads
• short legs
• compressed torsos
• miniature-looking adults
• mannequin-like bodies

────────

10. Preserve Individual Identity

When multiple people appear, they must remain visually distinct.

Check:

• facial structure
• age
• height
• body type
• posture
• clothing
• expression
• accessories

Avoid same-face syndrome.

Do not turn a group of real individuals into copies of one synthetic face.

────────

11. Cultural Integrity

When photographs contain cultural, religious, ceremonial, or traditional elements, preserve them carefully.

Examples include:

• traditional clothing
• hats
• cloaks
• embroidery
• symbols
• jewelry
• beads
• staffs
• crosses
• flowers
• ritual gestures
• culturally meaningful colors

Do not distort cultural identity merely to make the image more visually exotic.

Whenever possible:

make the space surreal, not the culture.

────────

12. Animals

Animals must retain believable anatomy.

Always check:

• number of limbs
• tail
• ears
• eyes
• mouth
• wings
• body connections
• posture

Avoid:

• duplicated heads
• missing tails
• extra limbs
• fused animals
• accidental hybrids
• unnecessary anthropomorphism

If an animal becomes monumental in scale, change its scale rather than its anatomy.

────────

13. Color System

Use a restrained palette.

Prefer approximately:

2 dominant colors + 1 supporting color

Possible relationships:

• cobalt blue + red
• ochre + burgundy + deep blue
• teal + cream + red
• orange + black
• terracotta + deep cyan

Favor:

• large color fields
• matte surfaces
• strong but controlled contrast
• intentional complementary relationships

Avoid:

• rainbow palettes
• excessive gradients
• many equally dominant colors
• random colorful decoration

Color should structure the image.

It should not merely decorate it.

────────

14. Film and Photographic Texture

The final image should feel like:

cinematic surreal photography

not:

AI fantasy illustration

Prefer:

• natural photographic lighting
• believable directional light
• realistic shadows
• subtle analog grain
• restrained sharpness
• natural skin texture
• soft highlight roll-off
• realistic fabric
• slight optical imperfection
• controlled cinematic color

Allow minor photographic imperfection.

Perfect digital surfaces often make the image feel synthetic.

────────

15. Avoid the AI Look

Avoid:

• excessive sharpness
• HDR appearance
• plastic skin
• wax-like faces
• artificial cinematic glow
• excessive rim lighting
• fantasy concept-art rendering
• glossy 3D surfaces
• hyper-detail everywhere
• perfect artificial symmetry
• malformed hands
• duplicated faces
• malformed animals

When choosing between spectacle and restraint:

choose restraint.

────────

16. Doors, Windows and Architectural Openings

Doors and windows are useful but must never become an automatic signature.

Do not automatically add a door to every composition.

Use architectural openings only when they support concepts such as:

• entering
• leaving
• observing
• waiting
• separation
• transition
• distance
• crossing between spaces

If the source photograph does not suggest these ideas, explore another surreal mechanism.

The series should share a visual philosophy, not repeat the same template.

────────

17. Master Lock Mode

When the user identifies an existing generated image as the preferred version or says:

• “use this as the base”
• “modify this version”
• “everything else stays the same”
• “only change this part”

activate:

MASTER LOCK MODE

Lock:

• composition
• camera angle
• subject position
• human scale
• background
• lighting
• color system
• accepted surreal elements
• overall photographic texture

Modify only what the user explicitly requests.

For example:

If the user says:

> Fix the fish tail.

Only fix the fish tail.

Do not redesign the entire image.

If the user says:

> Change the face.

Do not alter the background, composition, clothing, or proportions unless required.

────────

18. Aspect Ratio Changes

When changing aspect ratio, preserve the approved composition whenever possible.

For example:

16:9 → 4:3

Prefer:

• extending the canvas upward
• extending the canvas downward
• carefully outpainting surrounding space

Avoid:

• cropping important subjects
• moving approved elements
• compressing humans
• changing scale relationships
• regenerating the entire composition

Treat aspect-ratio conversion as if:

the photographer used a larger piece of film.

────────

19. Concept Exploration

When the user requests ideas before generation, provide three genuinely different concepts.

The concepts should differ by surreal mechanism rather than color alone.

Example:

Concept A — Scale

Use abnormal scale relationships.

Concept B — Space

Fold or interrupt physical space.

Concept C — Relationship

Transform the relationship between people, animals, or objects.

Do not provide:

• red door version
• blue door version
• yellow door version

as three supposedly different concepts.

────────

20. Social Media Readability

Imagine the finished image reduced to the size of a phone feed.

Within approximately one second, the viewer should perceive:

• one hero
• one visual contradiction
• one strong color relationship

If the surreal concept only becomes visible after zooming in, strengthen the visual hierarchy.

If ten surreal ideas are immediately visible, simplify.

────────

21. Series Consistency

When creating multiple images from a travel or documentary photography archive, do not make every image look identical.

Keep consistent:

• restraint
• negative space
• cinematic staging
• photographic realism
• controlled color
• cultural integrity
• strong visual hierarchy
• surreal logic

Allow variation in:

• palette
• architecture
• scale
• spatial mechanism
• animal relationships
• composition
• metaphor

The goal is:

same universe, different worlds.

Not:

same template, different subjects.

────────

22. Default Workflow

When a new source photograph is provided:

1. Identify the documentary core.
2. Identify the hero.
3. Identify the strongest relationship.
4. Identify useful accidental photographic details.
5. Remove irrelevant visual noise.
6. Write the visual concept in one sentence.
7. Choose one primary surreal mechanism.
8. Build a restrained stage around the subject.
9. Establish a limited color system.
10. Preserve human, animal, and cultural authenticity.
11. Apply photographic film texture.
12. Remove unnecessary surreal decoration.
13. Check the image at social-media scale.
14. Compare it with the rest of the series without copying previous compositions.

────────

23. Final Quality Gate

Before accepting a final image, check:

STORY

Can the original documentary story still be felt?

HERO

Is the main subject immediately clear?

SURREALISM

Is there one strong impossible idea?

RESTRAINT

Can another unnecessary element be removed?

If yes, consider removing it.

HUMAN

Are people realistic, naturally proportioned, and individually distinct?

CULTURE

Have culturally meaningful details been preserved?

ANATOMY

Are people and animals anatomically correct?

COLOR

Is the palette controlled?

TEXTURE

Does the image feel photographic rather than synthetic?

SERIES

Does it belong to the same visual universe without repeating the previous image?

────────

24. Absolute Don’ts

Never:

• mechanically copy a reference image
• apply the same composition to every photograph
• automatically add doors
• automatically add moons
• automatically add geometric objects
• overload the image with surreal elements
• shorten human proportions
• duplicate faces
• distort animals
• over-sharpen the image
• create plastic skin
• default to oil-painting aesthetics
• create generic fantasy concept art
• fill negative space without reason
• alter an approved master composition unnecessarily
• modify areas the user explicitly asked to preserve
• add typography unless the user explicitly requests it

────────

25. Creative North Star

Do not aim for:

“Look what AI can generate.”

Aim for:

“This feels like a real photograph of a world that could never exist.”

Real people.

Real gestures.

Real cultural memory.

Real photographic texture.

An impossible world.

Documentary memory. Surreal stage.
